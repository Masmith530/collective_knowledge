## What this is

This is real ground instruction, written by a working helicopter instructor —
an ATP and Gold Seal CFII — and sequenced the way it is actually taught. It is
meant to teach you the subject, not to summarise it.

What it is not is **official**. That distinction matters, and it is worth being
precise about rather than vague.

<Aside type="caution" title="Real instruction, not official authority">
Ground instruction here does not replace an authorized instructor's endorsement,
the Rotorcraft Flight Manual or Pilot's Operating Handbook for the aircraft you
are flying, or the current Federal Aviation Regulations. For anything you act on,
those are the authority and this is not.
</Aside>

Specifically:

- **This is not an FAA-approved course.** The instruction is real; the *approval*
  is what is missing. Reading this does not by itself satisfy a training, ground
  instruction, or endorsement requirement — only an appropriately rated and
  authorized instructor can provide and log those.
- **Do not use this material for flight planning or in-flight decisions.** Use
  current official sources.
- **Limitations, procedures, and performance numbers vary** by model, serial
  number, equipment, and manual revision. Where this site mentions a number, it is
  for illustration. The authoritative number for your aircraft is in your
  aircraft's flight manual.
- **Regulations change.** Where this site discusses a regulation, read the current
  regulation at [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/).
- **No warranty.** The content is provided as-is, without warranty of any kind.
  You are responsible for your own decisions as a pilot.

## Using this material

Short version: **yes, you can use it.**

Print a lesson for a student. Work through it on a whiteboard. Build your own
ground school on top of it. Hand it to a flight school. You do not need to ask
me first, and it does not matter whether you are charging for your instruction.

Two conditions:

- **Say where it came from.** Credit this site and link back to it.
- **Pass it on as freely as you got it.** If you adapt or build on it, your
  version has to carry the same permissions. You cannot take it, change it, and
  then fence it off.

That second condition is the one that matters, and it is the whole point. It
means this material cannot end up behind somebody else's paywall — including
mine. Anything built on it stays as open as it started.

Formally, the lessons, articles, quizzes and flashcards are licensed
[CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).
